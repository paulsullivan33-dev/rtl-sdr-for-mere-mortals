The lab — one recording, no antenna
========================================================================

This guide's lab is not a tree of files. It is a
single recording of the radio sky, plus a build
machine with no receiver plugged into it. Both
halves are deliberate, and both are stated here
so nothing in the chapters can pretend otherwise.

THE RECORDING
--------------------
modes1.bin — 713,736 bytes of unsigned 8-bit I/Q
samples at 2,000,000 samples per second, recorded
from a real antenna pointed at real 1090 MHz
aircraft traffic. It ships with the dump1090
project ("testfiles/") so the decoder can be
tested by people with no receiver at all. Get it
with fetch-sample.sh, beside this note; the
script verifies the SHA-256 of what arrives
(3a33e16025da8669149c780075950b4e908ca036ea21f9
583c113f60d5fb3094).

713,736 bytes is 356,868 I/Q pairs. At two
million pairs a second, the whole recording is
0.178 seconds of sky. Everything this guide
verifies by decoding comes out of that fifth of
a second. Chapter 5 counts what is in it: one
aircraft, ICAO address 4d2023, callsign AMC421,
217 usable messages.

THE BUILD MACHINE
--------------------
Ubuntu 24.04, no RTL-SDR dongle attached, no
antenna, no RF of any kind. Packages installed
from the Ubuntu archive purely to be run:

    rtl-sdr               2.0.1-2build1
    librtlsdr2            2.0.1-2build1
    dump1090-mutability   1.15~20180310.4a16df3+dfsg-8.1build1
    rtl-433               23.11-1build1
    sox                   14.4.2+git20190427-4build4

plus the original dump1090 (antirez) built from
its project source at build time, because its
own test file is the lab.

CONSEQUENCES, STATED ONCE
--------------------
Every command in this guide that needs a live
dongle was run anyway, and its real behaviour
with no dongle present is captured in
rtl-sdr-lab/transcripts/ ("No supported devices
found.", exit 1, in tool after tool). The
chapters teach from those captures what can
honestly be taught from them — command shapes,
defaults, planners, failure text — and mark
the rest as documented-only. Nothing anywhere
in this guide reports a signal the build
machine received, because it received none.

The one piece of live signal work is chapter
3's sox chain: a two-second tone synthesised
into the exact headerless shape rtl_fm emits,
then labelled and measured with sox. It proves
the audio plumbing, not the radio. The chapters
say so where they use it.
